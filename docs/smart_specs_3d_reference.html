🕶️ Smart Specs — 3D Modeling Reference

Component Dimensions, Placement & Layout Guide
For: 3D Printable Smart Glasses Frame (PLA/PETG)

1. Project Overview

A wearable smart glasses frame for a blind navigation aid. The glasses house a camera (center), an ESP32 microcontroller (right arm), speakers (both arms near ears), battery & power management (left arm). The frame must look like sporty sunglasses — not a bulky prototype.

Design Style: Wraparound sport sunglasses, matte black, 3D printed.
Lens openings: Present but open (no actual lenses).
Target weight: ~60-70g fully loaded with electronics.

6. Special Design Notes

FeatureDetails
ESP32 cradleOpen top — NO lid. Side snap rails hold the board. User must be able to lift it out and place back by hand.
USB-C port (TP4056)Must be flush with bottom or outer surface of left arm near hinge for easy charging access.
MT3608 screw accessSmall hole (3mm) in enclosure pointing upward, OR open-top design, so the voltage adjustment screw is reachable with a small screwdriver.
Speaker sound holes2-3 holes, 1.5mm diameter each, facing inward toward the ear canal.
Ventilation3-4 small holes (1mm) near ESP32 area for heat dissipation.
HingeSimple integrated friction hinge (print-in-place) or pin hinge.
Nose padsIntegrated into frame, angled 15° inward.

7. Signal & Power Flow

USB-C (charging) ↓ TP4056 (left arm) → charges → LiPo Battery (left arm) ↓ 3.7V MT3608 (left arm) → boosts to 5V ↓ 5V wire crosses bridge ESP32 (right arm) ← 5V power ↓ captures image via OV2640 camera ↓ sends via WiFi to Raspberry Pi (in pocket) ↓ receives detection result text ↓ DAC audio signal PAM8403 (right arm) ↙ ↘ Left Speaker Right Speaker (left ear) (right ear)
Wire count crossing the bridge:
• 2 wires: 5V + GND (from MT3608 on left → ESP32 on right)
• 2 wires: Left speaker audio + GND (from PAM8403 on right → speaker on left)
Total: 4 thin wires (26-28 AWG) through the cross-bridge channel.
